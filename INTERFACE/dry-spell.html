CASE STUDY · DISCHARGE ENSEMBLE & DRY SPELLS

River flow without
a gauge in the basin.

Thirty-eight CA-discharge gauges, monthly 6,739-record modelling with chronological holdouts, and the experiment that matters for ungauged basins: the same ensemble re-run with every discharge-derived feature removed — climate forcing, static basin attributes and seasonal terms only.

Two deliberately different models

Gauge-anchored nowcast

With discharge history

17/38

gauges with positive held-out R² (chronological 80/20). Best gauges 0.83–0.85; one at −1.34. Uses discharge lags, quantiles and baseflow — so it describes flows where a gauge already reports. It cannot move to a basin whose gauge is missing: its strongest inputs do not exist there.

Transfer experiment

Without any discharge feature

22/38

gauges positive; median R² 0.31. Climate forcing from upstream stations, static basin attributes and seasonal terms only. This is the model a basin without a gauge can actually use — and the foundation for dry-spell warning where discharge itself is the missing observation.

Why the distinction is the whole point. A model fed with lagged discharge predicts the river from the river. That is a legitimate nowcast at an instrumented site — and it is why its scores are higher. The transfer model is scored on the same held-out years with no discharge information anywhere in its inputs; every number it achieves is earned from data that exists before the water arrives.

Transfer results, per gauge

The best catchments lose almost nothing when discharge history is taken away — for them, climate and landscape already carry the signal. Regulated lowland gauges collapse: their flow is decided by reservoirs and canals, which no climate feature contains.

Dry spells: reconstruction now, warning next

A dry-spell month here means discharge below that gauge's Q25 — a relative low-flow proxy, not a drought-impact definition. On the 1,363 chronological held-out records, 24 dry-spell months were observed; mean absolute error during them is 2.65 m³/s against 10.31 m³/s in normal months. Low, stable flow is easier to describe than floods — so the model reconstructs dry periods well. That is reconstruction from concurrent conditions, not warning ahead of them.

Validated todayMonthly discharge reconstruction at 38 gauges under chronological holdouts; interval coverage 79.6% (below nominal 90% — stated, not sold as calibrated).

Known boundary. Regulated gauges break precipitation-to-flow reasoning; the Q25 proxy is relative, not an impact measure; and one split is one split — rolling-origin evaluation is specified before any forecasting claim.
