Evidence you can keep.

Three starting points for research and teaching with the public preview. These are reproducible navigation and download workflows, not claims of completed scientific validation.

01 / RESEARCH & TEACHING

What characterises
this catchment?

Useful for a first catchment assessment, a study-area description, or a GIS teaching exercise.

Take awayA basin profile with its identifier, selected attributes, units, and source references.

Try it with HYBAS 4120050220

  1. Open the basin explorer and zoom until the sidebar shows Level 12. Search for 4120050220 and select the result.
  2. Open Atlas attributes. Record local basin area, upstream catchment area, and the published characteristics relevant to your question.
  3. Read each attribute’s definition and units. Keep local and upstream quantities separate: upstream attributes already include the catchment above the basin and must not be added across basins.
  4. Download the basin JSON and shared catalogue so the attribute values retain their definitions.

02 / ENVIRONMENTAL ANALYSIS

How does the
monthly record vary?

Useful for exploring seasonal patterns and choosing a sufficiently complete period for subsequent analysis.

Take awayA monthly CSV and metadata, plus a record of which months and years are suitable for your comparison.

Check coverage before interpreting a pattern

  1. Select the same level-12 basin and open Monthly record. Choose precipitation, evapotranspiration, soil moisture, or runoff from the available variables.
  2. Inspect the 2003–2024 series and observed-month counts. Preserve the positions of null values; they represent missing observations.
  3. For annual totals, use only monthly flux quantities with all 12 months observed. Soil moisture is a state variable and should not be summed as an annual flux.
  4. Download the CSV and its metadata JSON. Record units, source release, method, and the period actually used.

Snow is withdrawn from trend analysis in this release. Increasing missing months are unresolved. A visible pattern alone is not evidence of a climate trend.

03 / METHODS & REPRODUCIBILITY

Why do two
estimates differ?

Useful for selecting a data source, understanding a disagreement, or preparing an independent reproduction study.

Take awayA comparison note describing both values and their methodological differences, with supporting metadata.

Compare definitions before comparing numbers

  1. Choose a level-12 basin, open Atlas attributes, and switch to Independent estimates.
  2. Find an attribute with a published estimate and expand its evidence row. If it is missing, choose another attribute; missing coverage is not a zero estimate.
  3. Record the original value and estimate alongside their source products, resolutions, periods, units, and methods. A blank difference may be intentional when quantities are not comparable.
  4. Explain whether the two values represent the same quantity. Download the basin JSON and catalogue to retain the evidence.

Agreement with HydroATLAS is not independent scientific reproduction. No atlas attribute has passed that gate in the public preview.
