Available work and future scope

A basin atlas you can inspect.

Public preview

The basin explorer is the launch product: published attributes, independent estimates, whole-catchment statistics over the upstream network, and a downloadable monthly record of fourteen variables for the Amu Darya and Syr Darya systems.

Thematic tools

Each of these reads the same 7,445 catchments from a different angle: the hydroclimate observatory for headwater snow, temperature and anomalies by elevation band; drought back to 1961 and Mann-Kendall trends across every basin; the land-cover observatory for annual 10 m change; hydrography for rivers, lakes and sub-basins; and withdrawals and deliveries, audited to the documents the figures came from.

Inspection tools

The data catalogue and attribute catalogue record where every dataset and attribute came from and on what terms. Data lineage separates what a source offers from what this project has fetched, published and verified. Layer review puts all 38 published geospatial layers on a map one at a time, with their schemas. The ontology and relationship tables expose the typed concepts, assertions and provenance that connect them.

Practical applications

The use-case guide walks through a basin profile, monthly record assessment, and source comparison with example downloads. These support research and teaching; they do not provide engineering design flows or operational forecasts.

Research evidence

Case studies present Chirchik and Pskem runoff modelling against station records, discharge estimation for catchments with no gauge, satellite altimetry over 378 lakes and reservoirs, and regional station–satellite comparison. These are supporting research, each with its own validation, its own limitations, and its negative results kept rather than dropped.

Dynamic HydroATLAS methods documents open-data estimates, their source resolutions and divergence from published attributes.

Next research priorities

Independently rerun attribute methods against pinned inputs, review comparisons against declared tolerances, and document the result. Investigate increasing missingness in snow records before restoring trend use. Keep each variable at the frontier its source offers, rather than at the date the portal last ran. Extend estimate coverage only with explicit source, period, resolution, and method evidence.

Longer-term development

Independent reproduction remains a scientific milestone. Reimplementation of the national environmental atlas and a monitoring ontology are future programmes, outside this preview. A complete 281-of-281 estimate count is not a launch requirement.

The research roadmap records these longer-term ambitions; its stages are not promises of features already available.
