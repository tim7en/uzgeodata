CASE STUDY · REGIONAL WATER BALANCE

Where the region’s water goes
and how much of that we actually know.

A water flow diagram for the Amu Darya and Syr Darya, adapted from the Water Flow Diagram method. Its published applications are municipal, where one utility can account for what it abstracted, delivered and lost. Nothing at that resolution is published for this region — so this is drawn at basin-system scale, every flow states how it is known, and the analysis that follows is built on the part that is measured.

The headline finding. Averaged over twenty-two years the region looks comfortable: it diverts about 62% of what precipitation less evapotranspiration generates. But the surplus varies by a factor of 2.83 between years while allocation does not vary at all — and in 2021 and 2023 the recorded diversion exceeded the surplus outright. Water was still delivered, which means it came from storage, snow and glacier melt, groundwater, or inflow from outside the accounting domain. A mean year is the one condition under which this system has room.

It does not close, and is not made to

This diagram does not close, and is not made to. Precipitation less evapotranspiration leaves a surplus larger than the recorded diversion, while the runoff series exceeds that surplus outright: two models on different grids disagree, and forcing the arrows to balance would hide exactly the thing worth seeing.

Basin systems, not municipalities. Withdrawal here is administered by country and canal system while the record is organised by catchment, and canal delivery crosses basin divides, so a national figure cannot be honestly disaggregated to a basin.

Sensitivity: what actually moves this system

The method’s published applications vary loss rates, connection rates and treatment capacity, because a utility has measured all of those. Almost none of it is measured here, so varying it would be varying assumptions and calling the spread a result. What is measured is the supply side — and that is where the sensitivity turns out to live.

Surplus ranges from 53.6 km³/yr (2021) to 151.5 km³/yr (2003), a factor of 2.83. Diversion is 38% of the surplus in the wettest year and 108% in the driest. Thin-margin years: 2008, 2017, 2018, 2020, 2021, 2023.

A twenty-two year slope is a statement about this record, not an attribution to climate change. Decadal variability alone can produce a decline of this size, and nothing here separates the two. What does not depend on that question is the operational finding: in the later half of the record the margin between what arrives and what is taken is repeatedly thin, whatever is causing it.

What the arithmetic says. The national plan’s own 10 per cent target, applied to agriculture, frees 5.26 km³/yr — enough to remove both years in which diversion exceeded supply, and to cut thin-margin years from 6 to 4. It does not eliminate them. Because agriculture is 91% of use, it is the only sector where a change of this size is arithmetically available at all: eliminating all municipal use would free less than a fifth of what a 10 per cent agricultural saving does.

What is deliberately not modelled

  • Distribution Losses. No baseline is published for this region. The method's own applications usually find this the largest single lever, and it cannot be evaluated here.
  • Groundwater Substitution. Abstraction is not published at a usable scale, so substituting groundwater for surface water cannot be represented.
  • Connection Rates. Not published. The wastewater inventory gives what 53 plants treat, not what share of generated wastewater reaches them.

What would have to change

For government bodies

  • ICWC / BWO Amu Darya & Syr Darya — publish diversion by basin and sector, not only by country. This is the precondition for almost everything else.
  • Ministry of Water Resources — establish a distribution-loss baseline. A 10 per cent reduction target cannot be evaluated against nothing.
  • State Committee on Geology — report groundwater abstraction, so a surface-water saving can be distinguished from a groundwater cost.
  • Uzsuvtaminot and vodokanals — publish wastewater generated alongside wastewater treated.

For academic partners

  • Digitise the ICWC series. One transcribed year is drawn here against twenty-two measured ones. The yearbooks hold annual limits and actuals; a machine-readable series is a contained, high-value piece of work.
  • Reconcile the products against gauges. Runoff and the climatic surplus disagree by more than the entire municipal sector. Uzhydromet records exist; this is answerable.
  • Attribute the decline. The surplus falls 1.96 km³/yr per year across this record. Whether that is decadal variability or a trend needs a longer record than 22 years.
  • Reproduce an attribute independently. Nothing in this atlas has passed independent reproduction; that gate is open to anyone.

Sources and limits

  • Interstate Commission for Water Coordination, Basin Water Organizations — BWO Amu Darya and BWO Syr Darya are the inter-ministerial bodies that set and record withdrawal limits and deliveries for the two basins. They are the administering authority, so their figures are what was allocated and diverted, not an independent measurement of what flowed.
  • CAWater-Info Yearbook 2022, Section 2: Water-related situation in the Aral Sea basin — Basin and country diversion figures for the 2022 hydrological year.
  • CAWater-Info Yearbook 2023, Section 5: Key water developments, Uzbekistan — National totals by source and the sectoral shares.
  • SIC ICWC analytical report 2023 — Context for reservoir inflow and basin conditions; not the source of any figure recorded here.
  • UzGeoData observation record — Precipitation, evapotranspiration and runoff, monthly for 7,445 level-12 basins, 2003-2024. Converted to volumes using published basin areas.
  • UzGeoData wastewater plant inventory — 53 plants with discharge in cubic metres per day, population served, treatment level and outfall basin.

Method reference. A methodology for integrated water balancing that connects WASH, IWRM and the human right to water and sanitation, visualising abstraction, distribution, consumption, wastewater and losses for a municipality so stakeholders can see where water goes. Developed with case studies including Bern and St Gallen (Switzerland), Tarapoto (Peru), Agadir (Morocco), Pespire (Honduras) and Santa Maria Bulacan (Philippines). — published applications.

Administered figures were transcribed by hand from published pages and are recorded under their own provenance class, because a transcription error there is not detectable by any automated check this project runs.
