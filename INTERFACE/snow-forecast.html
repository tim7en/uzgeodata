Forecasting the irrigation season from April snow

On 1 April, the snow lying in the mountains predicts much of the April–September river flow that irrigation depends on. This study tests that at every gauged catchment in the Aral basin with a long enough record, scoring each forecast only on years the model had not seen.

This work builds on research by Barnhart and colleagues at the U.S. Geological Survey and the Hydrometeorology Research Institute of Uzhydromet, who designed and tested vegetation-season streamflow forecasting from simulated snow in the Kashkadarya basin.
  • Barnhart, T.B., Umirzakov, G., Gafurov, A., Yarashev, D., Crowley-Ornelas, E., and Asquith, W.H. (in review). Snowpack and streamflow trends and seasonal forecasting of the Kashkadarya region, Uzbekistan. Submitted to Environmental Research Communications.
  • Barnhart, T.B., Drukker, O.A., Asquith, W.H., Crowley-Ornelas, E.R., Steeves, P.A., Yarashev, D., Gafurov, A., and Umirzakov, G. (2025). Simulated snow and meteorology variables, Qashqadaryo region, Uzbekistan, 1950–2023. USGS data release, doi:10.5066/P1IHVOVG.
  • Drukker, O.A., and others (2025). The Central Asia equal area projection and model grid. USGS, doi:10.5066/P13ECATC.
Their study uses SnowModel (Liston and Elder, 2006) at 100 m. This page applies the same forecast design with ERA5-Land snow, which is available for every basin, and reports only its own results.
1 · Pilot basin

Pskem at Mullala, 1951–2017

Four forecast dates, three ways of measuring skill. In-sample R² fits and scores on the same years. Cross-validated R² leaves each year out in turn. The operational score forecasts each year from earlier years only and compares it with the previous 30-year average, which is what a water manager would otherwise use.

4 · Method

How the forecasts are made and scored

Flow

CA-discharge 2023 ten-day records from 1932, averaged to months when all three ten-day values exist. For Pskem the Uzhydromet workbook adds 2016–2017; the two sources agree at r = 0.999 over 185 shared months.

Snow and weather

ERA5-Land on its native 0.1° grid, from 1950: snow water equivalent on the day before each forecast date, precipitation summed from 1 October, and monthly temperature. Cells are weighted by their overlap with each catchment and grouped into elevation bands. Cells that hold ERA5-Land's permanent glacier snow are left out of the snow predictors.

Scoring

  • In-sample: adjusted R² of the best model with up to three predictors, with a 1,000-sample bootstrap.
  • Cross-validated: each year left out in turn, with the predictor choice repeated inside every fold.
  • Operational: from 1971, each year forecast from earlier years only, shifted by the mean error of the previous ten years, and compared with the previous 30-year mean.

Limits

  • ERA5-Land's 9 km grid is coarse for small catchments; this is where the USGS 100 m SnowModel reanalysis should add skill.
  • Glacier melt and diversions are invisible to winter snow; glacier-fed rivers such as the Zeravshan score lowest.
  • Flow per unit of snow has fallen since the 1970s at several gauges; the ten-year correction follows that drift without explaining it.
