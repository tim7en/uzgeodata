THE PROJECT

A shared view of
connected water systems.

UzGeoData is a basin atlas for researchers investigating the Amu Darya and Syr Darya systems, including the headwaters beyond Uzbekistan.

Water in this region is made upstream and used downstream, usually in a different country. The atlas is built on that fact: its unit is the level-12 catchment, and every basin carries both its own values and the values of everything draining into it. Snowmelt in the Pamirs and the Tien Shan, a reservoir on the Syr Darya, and an irrigated district near the delta appear in one record, on one geometry.

It connects published HydroATLAS characteristics with independent estimates from open environmental data, and keeps what produced each number beside it: source release, method identifier, period, units and basin geometry version. Explore a catchment, inspect its monthly record, and take the values and their provenance into your own analysis.

The monthly record spans 288 calendar positions from January 2003, with fourteen variables per basin — precipitation, actual and potential evapotranspiration, runoff from two independent products, soil moisture, snow cover and snow water equivalent, minimum, mean and maximum temperature, climate water deficit, vapour pressure deficit and the Palmer drought severity index. How far each one reaches depends on its source rather than on the portal, and each says so.

WHAT THE ATLAS EXPLORES

Six questions the basin
is asked here.

Each one is a working tool over the same 7,445 catchments, not a separate dataset.

CATCHMENT RECORD

What is this basin, and what drains into it?

Published attributes beside independent estimates, the monthly record, and whole-catchment statistics that aggregate every basin upstream: area-weighted means, sub-basin extremes and water volumes.

Open the basin explorer ↗
HYDROCLIMATE

How do the headwaters behave?

Snow, temperature and precipitation where the water is made, by elevation band, with anomalies against the reference period for the mountain catchments that feed both rivers.

Hydroclimate observatory ↗
DROUGHT & TRENDS

Is it drier than it was?

Water-year drought and wet years back to 1961, Mann-Kendall and Sen slopes across every basin, and dry-spell modelling that estimates river flow for catchments with no gauge on them.

Trends ↗
RIVERS & STORAGE

Where does the water stand?

Rivers, lakes and level-12 sub-basins from HydroSHEDS, dams and their transboundary position, and satellite altimetry watching 378 lakes and reservoirs change level pass by pass.

Reservoirs from orbit ↗
LAND & PRESSURE

What is changing on the ground?

Annual 10 m land cover across the catchments, irrigated extent, population and settlement pressure — the demand side of a balance whose supply side is measured upstream.

Land-cover observatory ↗
WATER USE

Who takes it, and what reaches the delta?

Source-audited withdrawals, reservoir releases and deliveries down both rivers, assembled from reported figures with each one traced to the document it came from.

Withdrawals & deliveries ↗

Behind all six sits the same machinery: an ontology of typed concepts, datasets and assertions, a catalogue recording where every dataset came from and on what terms, a lineage view separating what a source offers from what this project has fetched and published, and a layer review that puts all 38 published geospatial layers on a map one at a time.

AVAILABLE NOW

Explore & inspect

Basin mapping across both systems, published attributes, independent estimates with variable coverage, whole-catchment statistics over the upstream network, and downloadable monthly records with their provenance.

NEXT RESEARCH PRIORITIES

Verify & strengthen

Independently reproduce attributes against pinned inputs, establish why snow observations go missing more often after 2017, and keep every variable at the frontier its source actually offers rather than at the date the portal last ran.

LONGER-TERM DIRECTION

Extend & connect

Versioned atlas editions, carefully assessed historical extensions, a national environmental atlas, and a monitoring ontology that links observation, method and place.

Research milestones depend on evidence and review. They are not release commitments. The record now reaches August 2026 for the variables whose sources reach that far, and December 2024 for the TerraClimate family, which has published nothing later; the portal does not fill that gap by extrapolation.

READ WITH CONTEXT

Traceable data.
Transparent limits.

Scientific status, citation & reuse →

This is a public research preview. No atlas attribute has passed independent scientific reproduction. The portal does not provide operational forecasts, engineering design flows, or allocation advice.

Coverage varies, by variable and by basin. A missing value is not zero, and the end of a series is the end of its source, not the end of the record: runoff and mean temperature run to August 2026, the TerraClimate variables stop at December 2024 because that is the latest month that product has published. Each series states where it ends and which runs produced it.

Two estimates are not two measurements. Sources differ in period, resolution and method, so agreement between them is not a reproduction test. Snow remains available for inspection but is withdrawn from trend analysis, because missing months increase across the record and the cause is not established.

Keep the metadata with your downloads and cite original datasets. Each source retains its own reuse terms.
